Everyone remembers the moment money stopped making sense. Maybe it was 2008 on the news. Maybe it was a grocery receipt that felt like a typo. Maybe someone at a barbecue wouldn't shut up about it. (Hi.)

What happens next matters more than people admit, because the bitcoin rabbit hole has *many* doors, and most of them are wrong. I know, because I went in through the worst one: the price chart. My own archive preserves the evidence — early posts breathlessly analyzing ratio indicators like a man reading tea leaves with a calculator. The price door leads to the casino lobby, where you learn nothing, feel everything, and usually leave poorer in both directions.

It took me years to find the rooms that mattered. So here's the tour I wish someone had handed me — the same one I now hand to friends, family, and random humanoids. Five stages, in order, with the exact doors.

## Stage one: the problem (before the solution)

Do not start with bitcoin. Start with the thing bitcoin answers, or none of it will stick.

The single best first door right now is Joe Bryan's **"What's the Problem?"** — one video, one sitting, no prior knowledge needed, and it builds the whole case from a thought experiment about a tiny island economy. It's the thing I send the genuinely curious. If your brain prefers a heavier meal, **Lyn Alden** is the gold standard: her *How Money and Banking Work* talk for one evening, or the book **Broken Money** if you want the full machinery — she explains the fiat plumbing so clearly that bitcoin becomes the obvious next thought instead of a leap.

You'll know stage one worked when you stop asking "why is bitcoin valuable?" and start asking "wait, why is a *dollar* valuable?" That inversion is the whole game.

## Stage two: the case

Now, and only now, bitcoin — argued properly.

**Vijay Boyapati's *The Bullish Case for Bitcoin***: the essay was Bitcoin 102 on the curriculum I built for my family in 2020, and it has survived every revision since. Still the cleanest single argument in the canon. Pair it with **Parker Lewis's *Gradually, Then Suddenly*** series, which patiently dismantles every objection you're currently forming. Then the books: **Saifedean's *The Bitcoin Standard*** for monetary history with teeth — read all of it, argue with some of it, that's allowed — and **Robert Breedlove** when you're ready for the philosophical flank.

Stage two ends with a strange symptom: the volatility stops scaring you, because you've stopped pricing the thing in the unit it's replacing. ([I wrote about that clock separately](/writing/moscow-time).)

## Stage three: the machine

You don't need this stage. Plenty of excellent bitcoiners never open the hood. But if your brain itches:

**learnmeabitcoin.com** is the best technical explainer on the internet — keys, transactions, blocks, all of it, in diagrams that actually respect you. Keep **mempool.space** open in the next tab and watch the real chain do the things you just read about; there's no substitute for seeing the heartbeat live. (A small version of it beats on this site's homepage.) For the layers above — Lightning, ecash, the whole settling-in-bitcoin stack — **Offchain Media** writes trust-model-first explainers that tell you plainly who holds what, which is the only question that matters up there.

## Stage four: the practice

Here's where reading ends and sovereignty begins — and where most tours stop one stage too early.

Knowledge without keys is a museum tour. Stage four is: get sats, get them into your own custody, and *train* — the $5 restore, the sweaty send, the drills I laid out in [the self-custody essay](/writing/self-custody-is-a-skill). If you want structure instead of improvisation, this is where the serious free curricula shine: **Looking Glass Education** (free-first, bitcoin-only, built by people I'd trust with my reading list) and **PlanB Academy** (six tracks, from your first sats to protocol depth). Either one will carry you further than a hundred more podcast episodes.

## Stage five: the mirror

The last stage isn't more information. It's Gigi's ***21 Lessons*** — the capstone on my 2020 curriculum and still the capstone now — which is not about how bitcoin works but about what falling down this hole *does to you*. You read it at the end because it only makes sense at the end, like the last page of a mystery you've been living in.

And then the secret nobody tells you: the rabbit hole is a loop, not a line. Re-read the Bullish Case after you've done your first solo restore and it's a different essay. Watch "What's the Problem?" after a year of stacking and you'll catch what it was really saying. Around here we just call that *going deeper*.

## Pick your door

One size fits nobody, so — the cheat sheet:

- **Your mom:** Joe Bryan's video. One sitting. Done.
- **The finance brain:** Lyn Alden, then Boyapati.
- **The engineer:** learnmeabitcoin.com with mempool.space in the second tab.
- **The philosopher:** Breedlove, then Gigi.
- **The structured learner:** Looking Glass or PlanB Academy, start to finish.
- **The 2019 version of me:** literally anything but the price chart.

The full annotated version of this list — every author, podcast, channel, site, and book I actually vouch for, sorted by level and kept current — now lives in [the Library](/library), which is the part of this site I expect to outlive everything else on it.

Welcome to the hole. Mind the first step; mine was a candlestick. ⚡
