Nobody's panicking about Bitcoin anymore. I want you to notice that, because it's the whole thing.

We're down about half from last October. Seven, eight months of grind. The price doesn't crash so much as *sag* — a little lower, a little more boring, week after week — and somewhere in there the crowd just… wandered off. The loud people already sold. The group chats went quiet. The replies under every Bitcoin post are either a eulogy or a guy who found a faster horse in some AI stock and wants you to know about it. One of the sharpest analysts I read has a name for this exact feeling: **time-pain**. Not a sharp crash — a slow war of attrition that bores you into giving up.

This is my favorite part.

## Two religions, one question

I don't trade Bitcoin. I accumulate it. Those aren't two styles of the same activity — they're two different religions.

A trader wakes up and asks: *is this the top or the bottom?* Exhausting, full-time, and the market is built to separate that person from their coins.

An accumulator asks exactly one question: *how hard should I be buying right now?* The answer is never "sell." It just slides between "normal" and "back up the truck." When everyone else is bored and angry and gone — the dial goes to **truck.**

Blood in the streets was never the risk. It's the discount.

## Why I can watch this calmly

I don't own Bitcoin as a bet on a cycle. I own it because I think it's a moral imperative — the first genuinely sound money in a world that debases everything else on purpose — and the best store of value humanity has built for a digital age. I'm not renting that view until the next rally. I hold it.

So when the price sags, my thesis doesn't move. Only the discount does.

## Four gauges, one answer

I'm not asking you to buy the vibes — I did the homework, and it's all laid out, chart by chart, in [*The Grinding Bottom*](https://open.substack.com/pub/radvladdy/p/the-grinding-bottom), the deep-dive version of this piece on Substack. Four independent ways of measuring "how cheap is Bitcoin, historically" — cost basis, capital flows, who's underwater, and pure time — and right now all four read the same square: the bottom decile.

![Four independent gauges — cost basis, risk score, power-law position, and holder-loss statistics — all pinned in the deep-value zone at once](/images/writing/i-dont-trade-bitcoin-i-stack-the-blood/four-gauges.png)

### Three from on-chain
These three share almost no inputs, and they agree anyway.

- **Cost basis** — a blended read of how far price has strayed from where the market's average buyer sits. It's in the bottom decile of Bitcoin's whole history — only about one day in ten, ever, has been cheaper. And the coins moving right now aren't the old hands capitulating: just 7% of last month's activity came from wallets that bought below $60k. The sellers are people who bought the recent top and finally cracked. Long-term holders now control 78% of everything ever mined — a share that only gets this high late in a bear, as coins migrate from weak hands to strong ones.
- **Capital flows** — real dollars are still leaving the network, so this is a genuine bear, not a headfake. But the *rate* has collapsed, from roughly a billion dollars a day a few weeks ago to under a hundred million now. And this sell-off is institutionally led — ETF outflows, not retail panic, account for about a quarter of the whole decline.
- **Who's underwater** — the share of long-term holders sitting on a paper loss. It just posted ~45%, its second-highest reading ever recorded at a bottom, beaten only by the 2015 low.

Cheap, sellers thinning out, old hands still holding. That's what a bottom looks like while it's happening, not after.

### The fourth: pure time
This is the one that got my attention most — the **power law**, the single model that's tracked Bitcoin's whole life, reading nothing but time and blind to everything else. I fit it myself: the exponent comes out to 5.62, and it explains 96% of the variance across sixteen years and four cycles. Today, Bitcoin is sitting *right on its support line* — 0.48× fair value, the cheapest ~11% of its entire history relative to trend.

![Bitcoin's full price history on the power-law corridor — price hugging the lower support band, in the cheapest decile relative to trend](/images/writing/i-dont-trade-bitcoin-i-stack-the-blood/powerlaw-corridor.png)

Four instruments, none sharing parts, all reading "deep value" at once. That's rare. It doesn't mean the exact bottom is today — bottoms are a *season*, not a day, and this one's most likely grinding out over the coming months. It means we're in the stretch where patient buyers have historically been rewarded on a multi-year horizon. No promises, just base rates.

### The objection worth taking seriously
Michael Howell, the sharpest liquidity analyst I read, tracks the tide of money moving through the global financial system, and his read is bearish. His framework is a ~65-month global liquidity cycle, and Bitcoin, he says, is one of the purest barometers of it — liquidity leads price by about 13 weeks. That liquidity peaked in late 2025 and is tightening now, partly on a wall of government debt coming due in 2026–27 that has to be refinanced, draining cash out of every risk asset, Bitcoin included. Taken alone, that says the bottom could land later, and lower, than the four gauges above suggest.

But follow his own logic one step further — you can't refinance that much debt without the printing presses eventually turning back on to make room for it. Howell's own phrase for it: *"the great debasement is still to come."* The pressure squeezing Bitcoin now is the same thing that forces the next flood of liquidity, and his liquidity turn lines up eerily well, on his own clock, with where the four gauges already point.

## The one thing to do first

Do **not** buy a single sat yet — not to trade, not even to accumulate — until you've done the work to understand what Bitcoin actually is and why it matters.

The reason I can watch a 50% drawdown and feel *opportunity* instead of nausea isn't willpower. It's conviction, and conviction isn't a mood — it's built slowly, by understanding the money you're holding. You can't buy it and you can't fake it.

And here's the cruel mechanic: the volatility *finds* everyone who skipped that step. It waits for the ugliest moment, then quietly relieves them of their coins — usually right at the bottom, to people who did the reading. Every cycle, without fail.

So if you're tempted to start: start with the understanding, not the buying. Read the book. Run the node. Sit with the *why* until it's load-bearing. Then open the dial.

## So, am I buying?

Yeah. Quietly, on a schedule, more when it hurts more. Not because I know where the bottom is — nobody does, and anyone who tells you a price and a date is selling something. I'm buying because I did the work a long time ago, the thesis hasn't changed, and the asset I believe is a moral imperative is on sale while everyone else looks the other way.

The good stuff comes to the people who understood it early enough — and held tightly enough — to still be standing here when nobody else is.
