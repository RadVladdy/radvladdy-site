In January 2019 I started a blog with one sentence in it: welcome to a site dedicated to helping "family, friends, and random humanoids understand and safely invest in bitcoin."

*Random humanoids.* I was very proud of that.

The plan was simple. The people I love were watching the financial world get stranger by the month, and I was convinced I'd found the exit. All I had to do was explain it. So I built them a curriculum — Bitcoin 101 through Bitcoin 401, like a university nobody had asked to attend. I posted articles. I posted podcasts. I posted price models with charts that went up and to the right.

And in April 2020, when the moment came for the big one — the heartfelt letter, the *this is why, from me, to you* — I choked.

I didn't write it. I posted someone else's.

## The letter I outsourced

On April 27, 2020, a pseudonymous writer named Gigi published "Dear family, dear friends" — a letter to everyone he loved who still had no bitcoin. I posted it the day he published it.

It opened with two convictions: that our money is fundamentally broken, and that using superior money would benefit the people he loved — and society — in the long run. It pointed at governments conjuring trillions — *"with a T"* — out of thin air, and asked the question that question marks were invented for: if they can just make more of it, what exactly is it that you're saving?

It said everything I had been fumbling toward for a year, in half the words and twice the warmth. So I hit publish on his words, attached Guy Swann's narration for the family members who'd rather listen than read, and told myself that counted as writing to them.

It half counted.

## Report-card time

Five years and two halvings later, let's grade everything I put in front of my family — the letter, the price model I kept amplifying next to it, the buying advice, and me.

**Gigi's letter: A+.** It was written in the exact month the first trillions were being conjured. The trillions did not stop. The broad money supply grew roughly 40% in the following two years, and the bill arrived in 2022 as the worst inflation in four decades. The letter reads less like a warning now and more like a weather report. This is why you should be suspicious of anyone who grades their own book recommendations: I'm giving an A+ to the thing I chose to send. But some things age so well the grade stops being an opinion.

**The price model I kept posting: C.** In February 2020 I published breathless podcast notes about stock-to-flow — the model predicting $50,000 to $100,000 bitcoin *right after* the May 2020 halving. Here's what the universe actually served: anyone who took my post's title literally — it contained the word *ASAP*, in February 2020, with bitcoin around $9,000 — got to experience March 12, 2020 within six weeks, when it crashed below $4,000 in a single day. It then took a year and a half to reach $69,000 — inside the predicted range, far outside the predicted schedule — and then spent 2022 visiting $15,000, which was in nobody's model. Direction: right. Precision: fantasy. The lesson I actually learned: **never hand your family a price target. Hand them the why.** A price target comes with an expiration date. The why compounds.

**My "where to buy" advice: B−.** Of the venues I recommended in 2020, only one would survive my standards today — the bitcoin-only one. The others taught me a rule I now apply everywhere: companies that sell bitcoin *and* five hundred other coins are casinos with a bitcoin booth in the lobby. Send your family to businesses that do one thing.

**The curriculum: A.** Boyapati's *Bullish Case*. Parker Lewis. Breedlove. Gigi's *21 Lessons* as the graduate seminar. Five years of chaos later, that's still the canon — most of it is still on my Library page today. Past me had taste; he just buried it under price charts.

**Me: incomplete.** Because the assignment was never the links. The people I love didn't need a syllabus. They needed me to say the thing in my own voice and sign my own name to it — and I outsourced that part for five years.

So. Better late.

## The letter, five years late

Dear family, dear friends —

I'm not writing to ask you to buy anything. I'm writing because there's something I understand about money that I'd want you to understand even if bitcoin didn't exist.

The money in your bank account is a promise. It works only as long as the people running the system keep the promise — and for your entire adult life, they have been quietly breaking it. Every crisis gets solved the same way: make more money. The cost never lands on the people who made it. It lands on the people holding the old units — on savers. On you. That's not a conspiracy theory; it's printed in the money-supply chart, and you've felt it every year at the grocery store.

Bitcoin is the first savings technology that doesn't require trusting a promise-maker. There will only ever be 21 million. Nobody can make more — not for a war, not for an election, not for a bank that gambled badly. That one boring property changes everything about saving.

You don't need to understand all of it. I still don't, and I run the machines. Here's all I'd actually ask: start smaller than feels meaningful, so the price can't scare you. Ignore the number for four years; it will do something stomach-turning in both directions, and none of it will matter. Eventually, learn to hold it yourself — it's easier than you think, and I will sit with you while you do it.

Worst case, you spent one evening and a little pocket money learning how money actually works — which is more than anyone else will ever teach you.

I'm not asking you to believe me. I'm asking you to read for one evening and then ask me anything.

Love,
the guy with the laser eyes

## Verify, don't trust (even me)

Everything I described is still in [this site's archive](/archive) with its original 2020 dates — the curriculum, the breathless stock-to-flow notes, Gigi's letter, all of it, kept up exactly so you can check my homework. That's the house rule here: receipts or it didn't happen.

And if you're not the bitcoiner in your family — if you're the sibling or the parent who just got sent this post by the one with the laser eyes — I'll translate: this is how we say *I worry about you and I love you.* We can't help it.

The letter above is for you too. ⚡
