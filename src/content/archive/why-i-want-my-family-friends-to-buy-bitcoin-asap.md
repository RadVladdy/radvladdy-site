<h4><strong>How PlanB Got Into Bitcoin</strong></h4>



<ul><li>PlanB is currently based in Amsterdam where he works for a multi-billion dollar international fund focused on traditional assets</li><li>He became interested in Bitcoin because of its supply cap of 21 million coins<strong>; it’s the first scarce digital object the world has ever seen</strong><ul><li><strong>Surely, this digital scarcity has value, but how much? </strong>– To find an answer, PlanB decided to apply his expertise to perform solid statistical econometric analysis </li></ul></li></ul>



<h4><strong>Valuing Bitcoin Based on its Stock-to-Flow Ratio</strong></h4>



<ul><li>In <em><a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://amzn.to/2t0Lz7k" target="_blank">The Bitcoin Standard</a></em>,
 author Saifedean Ammous describes the scarcity of a commodity in terms 
of its Stock-to-Flow (SF) ratio; if you’re unfamiliar with 
Stock-to-Flow:<ul><li>The Stock (S): the existing supply of a commodity</li><li>The Flow (F): extra production in the next period</li><li><strong>Stock-to-Flow: How much extra is produced compared to existing stockpiles – (S/F</strong>)<ul><li><strong>Currently, Bitcoin has a stock of 18,000,000 coins and a yearly flow of 750,000 new coins—an SF ratio of 27</strong></li></ul></li></ul></li><li><strong>PlanB discovered the Stock-to-Flow ratio of all commodities is correlated with their market value</strong><ul><li>For instance,<strong> the SF ratio of gold is 55 and its market value is $10 trillion </strong></li><li><strong>“If
 you plot the SF ratio of all commodities against their values, you get a
 straight line—it’s actually a power law with super high R<sup>2</sup> correlation”</strong> – <em>PlanB</em><ul><li>If you’re unfamiliar with R<sup>2</sup>, it measures the quality of fit for a model on a 0-100% scale</li></ul></li></ul></li><li>PlanB applied two models to test the correlation between Bitcoin’s Stock-to-Flow ratio and price:<ul><li>The cross-asset model, which resulted in an R<sup>2</sup> value of 99.5%<ul><li>“It’s amazing; I have never, ever seen that before” – <em>PlanB</em></li></ul></li><li>The time series model, which resulted in an R<sup>2</sup> value of 95% </li><li><strong>These above values indicate Bitcoin’s</strong> <strong>price is closely linked to the halving function</strong> (discussed below)</li></ul></li></ul>



<h4>The <strong>Bitcoin Halving Function</strong></h4>



<ul><li>The halving is a significant Bitcoin event: The supply rate of new coins is halved every 210,000 blocks (roughly every ~4 years)</li><li><strong>This halving doubles the Stock-to-Flow ratio and increases Bitcoin’s value</strong><ul><li>Currently, Bitcoin has an SF ratio of 27 – the model estimates the price to be little under $10,000 per bitcoin</li></ul><ul><li><strong>The next halving in May 2020 will increase its value by 8-10x, resulting in an estimated Bitcoin price of $50,000-100,000</strong></li><li><strong>The halving during  2024 will double the SF again to 100, resulting in a Bitcoin price of ~$400k – 1MM </strong> </li></ul></li><li><strong>A Stock-to-Flow ratio of 100 is something humanity has never seen before</strong>. and is made possible with a decentralized/fixed supply of a digital token<ul><li>Preston comments that <strong>you can keep mining more gold out of the ground, but with Bitcoin, its absolute scarcity, thus tightening the halving function</strong></li></ul></li><li>“People
 underestimate the speed at which this is going; [Bitcoin’s] SF ratio 
will double to near gold levels after May 2020, which means the market 
cap of Bitcoin will be in the trillions of dollars—$1-2 trillion, 
compared to the USD monetary base of $3 trillion. That’s an order of 
magnitude in the same range. In 2024, [assuming Bitcoin’s] SF ratio 
doubles to 100, its market cap will be in the $10-20 trillion range, way
 above the USD, which has all kinds of implications.” – <em>PlanB</em></li></ul>



<h4><strong>Bitcoin’s Path to a $10 Trillion Dollar Market Cap</strong></h4>



<ul><li><strong>Hitting the $10-20 trillion market cap will change what 
people use as a unit of account—it wouldn’t make sense to price things 
in dollars anymore</strong></li><li>Bitcoin’s current market cap is only $150 billion, compared to $500 trillion for derivatives and $200 trillion for bond markets</li><li><strong>Many
 traditional markets (like stock and real estate markets) have a 
monetary premium beyond the fundamental value they hold; they’ll likely 
lose that premium to the asset that captures and holds it best (Bitcoin</strong>)</li></ul>



<h4><strong>The Implications of a Bitcoin Futures Market Settled in Bitcoin</strong></h4>



<ul><li><strong>Derivatives are important—they allow for more liquid 
markets that attract bigger institutional investors. Bitcoin has several
 big futures markets.</strong><ul><li>A futures contract lets you buy 
and sell bitcoins at a predetermined price at a specified time in the 
future, settled in the future</li></ul></li><li>CME Bitcoin futures are settled in cash – you could theoretically sell more than 21 million bitcoins, even if they don’t exist</li><li>Bakkt
 Bitcoin futures are settled in physically delivered bitcoins – to sell 
bitcoins, you need to actually have them (and that changes the whole 
game)</li><li>There can be arbitrage between Bakkt and CME, and investors will be more cautious shorting Bitcoin<ul><li>If the price goes up, they’ll need to deliver those bitcoins—it’s not a matter of putting up more fiat</li></ul></li></ul>



<h4><strong>The Significance of the Sharpe Ratio When Investing in Bitcoin</strong></h4>



<ul><li><strong>Bitcoin has a Sharpe ratio above 1, which indicates the average risk is smaller than the average return</strong>; this is hard to see in assets<ul><li>Bitcoin’s average return is above 200% per year, while the worst loss is 80%</li><li>(The Sharpe ratio is a measure of risk-adjusted return)</li></ul></li><li>Using the <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://www.investopedia.com/articles/trading/04/091504.asp" target="_blank">Kelly Criterion</a>, just like in poker, you size your bet depending on your odds</li><li><strong>“If
 you invested just 1% of your portfolio in Bitcoin and let the other 99%
 do nothing—if you kept it cash, or something similar— that would have 
outperformed the S&amp;P 500 over a 4-year period for all years in the 
past 6, 7, or 8 years</strong>” – <em>PlanB</em></li><li><a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://podcastnotes.org/2017/09/19/the-tim-ferriss-show-ray-dalio-the-steve-jobs-of-investing/" target="_blank">Ray Dalio</a> explains how his investing revolves around two principles:<ul><li>Finding something with a very high Sharpe ratio</li><li>Mixing it with 10-15 uncorrelated positions that also have high Sharpe ratios</li></ul></li></ul>



<h4><strong>Bitcoin Has Yet to Be Tested By a Financial Crisis</strong></h4>



<ul><li>Bitcoin was invented in 2009 as a response to the 2008 financial crisis</li><li>Inverted
 yield curves and other measures are pointing to an incoming 
recession—this will be the time to test Bitcoin for negative correlation
 with other assets <ul><li><strong>If Bitcoin proves to be inversely 
correlated with traditional assets during a financial crisis, it’ll make
 it an undeniable safe-haven asset</strong></li></ul></li><li><strong>“The negative interest on $17 trillion dollars worth of bonds together with quantitative easing is the reason I’m in Bitcoin” – </strong><em>PlanB</em></li><li>PlanB mentions the <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2019/04/29/Enabling-Deep-Negative-Rates-A-Guide-46598" target="_blank">IMF report</a> proposing deep negative interest rates (-5 to -10%) </li><li><strong>Negative-yielding bonds mean that buyers holding the securities to maturity are guaranteed to make a loss</strong><ul><li>Preston
 explains that making a profit on a negative-yielding bond means buyers 
expect the interest rates to go even lower; the only way to make money 
is by finding a greater fool willing to take an even bigger loss—it’s 
the definition of the greater fools theory of investing (a Ponzi scheme)</li><li><strong>Most investors are starting to see the unsustainability of this and more will hedge against it with Bitcoin</strong></li></ul></li></ul>



<h4><strong>Hyperinflation &amp; Bitcoin:</strong></h4>

 U.S. has too much to lose by giving up its reserve currency status;
